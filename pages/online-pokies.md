## How online pokies actually work

### The random number generator

Every spin is decided by an RNG that is generating numbers continuously, whether anyone is playing or not. When you press spin, the game takes whichever numbers exist at that microsecond and maps them to reel positions. Two consequences follow, and both matter. First, the result was fixed before the reels finished their animation — the spinning is presentation, not process. Second, **the machine has no memory**. A pokie that has not paid in 300 spins is exactly as likely to pay on spin 301 as it was on spin one. There is no "due".

### Certification, and what it actually certifies

RNGs from licensed studios are tested by independent laboratories — eCOGRA, iTech Labs, GLI. The certificate covers the *game build*: that the RNG is statistically random and that the game returns its stated RTP over millions of rounds. It does not certify the casino. This is the distinction that matters most: a certified game can sit in the lobby of an operator that refuses withdrawals. Game fairness and operator trustworthiness are separate questions, which is why we test payouts rather than just checking badges.

### Paylines, ways and clusters

Classic pokies pay on fixed lines running across the reels. "Ways" games — 243 ways, 1,024 ways — pay for matching symbols on adjacent reels regardless of position. Megaways varies the symbol count per reel each spin, producing up to 117,649 combinations. Cluster games drop the concept of lines entirely and pay for groups of touching symbols. None of these mechanics is inherently better value; they change how wins arrive, not how much comes back.

### Wilds, scatters and how features trigger

A **wild** substitutes for other symbols to complete a win. A **scatter** pays or triggers a feature regardless of position, which is why three scatters anywhere usually starts free spins. Most of a modern pokie's RTP is concentrated in its feature rather than its base game — often 60% or more — which is precisely why high-volatility games feel dead until they suddenly do not.

## Pokie types, and who each suits

### Classic three-reel

Three reels, a handful of lines, no feature to wait for. RTP often 95–97% and volatility low. The best choice if your goal is to make a small budget last an evening.

### Video pokies

Five reels, free spins, multipliers, the default modern format. RTP 94–96.5%, medium volatility. Where most players should start.

### Megaways

Big Time Gaming's licensed engine, now everywhere. Up to 117,649 ways, high volatility, long dry spells. Needs a bankroll of several hundred times your spin to play properly.

### Cluster pays and cascading reels

Wins remove symbols and new ones drop in, chaining reactions. Feels generous, and the chain mechanic disguises the fact that the RTP is the same as anything else.

### Bonus buy

## RTP: the number that decides how much you keep

Return to player is the percentage of all wagered money a pokie pays back over millions of spins. A 96% pokie returns NZ$96 for every NZ$100 staked across its lifetime; the missing NZ$4 is the house edge. It says nothing about your next session — variance dominates in the short run — but over a year of regular play, the difference between a 94% game and a 97% game is the difference between an expensive hobby and a cheap one.
